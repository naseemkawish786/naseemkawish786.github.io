Pet Care Basics: Is Your Dog or Cat Sick?

Pets cannot tell us when something hurts. They show us instead — in small changes that are easy to miss. Here are the signs every pet parent should know, the protection every pet needs, and the calm first steps to take.

▶

Warning signs your pet may be sick

  • Not eating for more than a day (cats especially should never skip meals for long).
  • Unusual tiredness — hiding, sleeping much more, or losing interest in play.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea that lasts more than 24 hours, or contains blood.
  • Coughing, sneezing or hard breathing that does not quickly pass.
  • Sudden weight loss or gain, drinking far more water than usual.
  • New lumps, wounds that do not heal, limping, or crying when touched.
  • Behaviour changes — aggression, confusion, or clinginess in a normally independent pet.

Any one of these is your pet asking for help. Trust your instincts: you know your pet's normal better than anyone.

Vaccination — the invisible shield

Vaccines protect pets from diseases that are common, contagious and sometimes deadly. Core vaccines for dogs usually include rabies, distemper and parvovirus; for cats, rabies and the FVRCP combination. Puppies and kittens need a starter series, and adults need boosters on the schedule your vet recommends. Keep a simple record of every vaccination date — it is your pet's health passport.

Simple first steps when you notice a sign

  • Write it down: when it started, what changed, and how your pet is eating, drinking and toileting.
  • Keep them comfortable: warm, quiet rest, fresh water nearby, and no new foods or home medicines.
  • Call a veterinarian and describe what you wrote down. For breathing trouble, collapse, seizures, or suspected poisoning, go immediately — do not wait.
  • Follow up: give only the medicine the vet prescribed, exactly as directed.

⚠️ This article shares general pet care information for education only — it is not veterinary advice. If your pet shows any worrying sign, please see a qualified veterinarian. Early treatment saves pets, and it usually costs far less than waiting.
