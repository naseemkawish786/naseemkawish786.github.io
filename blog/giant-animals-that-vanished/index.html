Giant Animals That Vanished From Earth

Today the blue whale is the biggest animal on Earth. But long ago, the planet belonged to giants so huge that even whales would look nervous. Three of them star in our video — here are their true stories.

▶

Megalodon — the shark longer than a bus

The megalodon was the largest predatory shark that ever lived, reaching an estimated 15 to 18 metres — longer than a city bus. Its teeth were up to 18 centimetres long, like steak knives, and fossil teeth are still found on beaches today. Megalodon ruled the oceans for over 20 million years before vanishing about 3.6 million years ago, as the oceans cooled and its food supply changed.

Tyrannosaurus rex — the strongest bite on land

T. rex stood about 12 metres long with banana-sized teeth and the strongest bite of any land animal ever measured — strong enough to crush bone. For 66 million years its fossils slept underground; then an asteroid ended the age of dinosaurs in a single catastrophe. Birds, remarkably, are its living relatives — which means a tiny piece of the dinosaur story still sings outside your window.

Woolly mammoth — the elephant in a winter coat

The woolly mammoth was an elephant built for the Ice Age: a thick fur coat, small ears to keep the heat in, and curved tusks up to 4 metres long for sweeping snow away from grass. Most mammoths died out as the world warmed about 10,000 years ago — but a last small herd survived on Wrangel Island until only about 4,000 years ago, when the pyramids of Egypt were already standing.

Why did the giants vanish?

Each giant fell for a different reason: an asteroid for the dinosaurs, a cooling ocean for the megalodon, and a warming world — plus human hunters — for the mammoth. It is a reminder that even the biggest animals depend on a stable home. Protecting today's giants, like the elephants and whales in our long videos, matters more than ever.
